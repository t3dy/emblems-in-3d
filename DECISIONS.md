## 2026-09-04 — Phase 7: *The Fugitive World*, one walkable world of 51 stations

**The ask.** One coherent three.js world — in the spirit of `../HPin3D` — that tours
all 51 emblems of *Atalanta Fugiens* in sequence, with free walking and a guided tour,
carrying Maier's own text, de Jong's readings, and summaries of the alchemical and
mythological sources. Built with the
[Threejs-Awesome-Graphics-Agent-Skills](https://github.com/scottstts/Threejs-Awesome-Graphics-Agent-Skills)
pack, which is installed here as `$threejs-*` skills.

### 1. The world's organising conceit is Maier's own, not an invented hub

The book *is* a race: Hippomenes chases Atalanta, the reader chases fleeting truths,
and the fifty fugues are all canons of pursuit. So the connective tissue is **the
course** — a single road walked in emblem order, from the title-page start line to
Emblem L. No invented lobby, no museum. Rejected alternatives: a hub-and-spoke gallery
(reads as a menu, not a world) and four separate stage-districts (would force a clean
NIGREDO→RUBEDO partition the book does not have — see §3).

### 2. Two tiers of station, and the difference is *visible*

Phase 6's armature router established that **only 5 of 51 plates have a recoverable
horizon**; 46 honestly return "no horizon recoverable". The world does not paper over
that.

| tier | plates | what you walk into |
|---|---|---|
| `measured` | the 4 admissible plates | a **room**: metric diorama built from `Z = f·E/(y−h)`, cutouts at their measured ground contacts, the station point marked on the floor. The reprojection gate still applies inside it. |
| `conjectural` | the other 47 | a **flat**: the plate as a standing cut sheet with its cutouts popped forward at a nominal papercraft offset, in *parallel* projection so no perspective claim is made. Labelled as conjecture in the world and in the panel. |

This is deliberate. The project's progress becomes legible terrain: as review work
converts a plate from conjecture to measurement, a flat becomes a room. It also means
the world can ship now without inventing 46 sets of numbers.

### 3. Stage colour is a continuous gradient, not four districts

The stages are not monotonic in emblem order (III is ALBEDO, IV–V back to NIGREDO,
X is CITRINITAS, XI–XIII ALBEDO again; RUBEDO only runs clean from XXVII). Rather than
reorder the book or lie about the partition, ground tint, fog colour and light
temperature **lerp along the road** toward each station's own stage palette
(`emblems.stage_palette` in the Claudiens DB). Walking the course therefore feels the
oscillation the book actually has.

### 4. Each station sits in a *place*, generated, not in a void

`locations.js` already assigns every emblem a setting archetype read off Maier's own
text (Emblem III's washerwoman → riverside, VI's sowing → farm, XXII's "do woman's
work, that is to say: cook" → kitchen). Phase 7 promotes that from a background-prop
list to generated period architecture and landscape via
`$threejs-procedural-architecture`, so the laboratories, farms, kitchens, courtyards,
temples and castles the emblems are set in are actually built.

### 5. Built geometry is shaded with Merian's own strokes

The one hard visual requirement: a built wall must not look like plastic standing next
to a burin cutout. Phase 6 §3 called for a tonal-art-map hatching shader whose hatch
tiles are **sampled from the plates themselves**. That is now the world's only
material, applied to every generated surface, so built and drawn geometry are the same
substance. Via `$threejs-procedural-materials`.

### 6. Text comes from the database, never retyped

`site/data/world.json` is generated by `tools/build_world.py` from
`C:\Dev\Claudiens\db\atalanta.db` (mottos LA/EN, epigrams, discourse summaries,
scholarly refs keyed to de Jong 1969 with page, and the `source_authorities` rows that
name the alchemical and classical texts each discourse cites), joined to this repo's
`data/perspective.json`, `data/elements.json` and `site/assets/manifest.json`. Nothing
in the narration is authored in the renderer. Every DRAFT/AI-assembled row keeps its
provenance flag into the panel.

### 7. Camera: one projection owner, two modes

Free walk (pointer-lock) and the guided tour share **one** camera whose projection is
owned by the world, *except* inside a `measured` station's station point, where the
camera's `f` and eye height are set from that plate's solve so the gate holds. Mode
handoffs are quaternion blends, per `$threejs-camera-direction`. Getting this wrong
would silently break the one thing Phase 5 bought.


### 8. A solve is only admitted if the lens it implies is one a person could draw through

**2026-09-04, found while building the tour.** Emblem XXIV's automatic
two-vanishing-point solve reports *f* = 197 px on a 1408 px plate. The guided tour
dutifully set the camera to it and produced a **149° vertical field of view** — a
fisheye. No perspective construction in this corpus was drawn through such a lens; the
orthogonality fit `f² = −(v₁−c)·(v₂−c)` is simply spurious there.

`tools/build_world.py` now gates admission to the measured tier on the implied vertical
field of view falling in **15°–70°** (the three hand-measured plates sit at 27.8°, 43.3°
and 43.4°). A rejected plate falls back to the conjectural tier and its panel says so,
with the number and the reason. It is **not** clamped: clamping would turn a bad
measurement into a plausible-looking one, which is the exact failure Phase 5 was built
to stop.

Consequence: the world is 4 measured / 47 conjectural, not 5 / 46, and
`data/perspective.json` now has a known-bad entry worth fixing in the review app.


### 9. Three ways through, one terrain

**2026-09-04.** The road is laid out in emblem order because that is the book.
A *route* does not move a station; it chooses the order the tour visits them.

- **Emblem order** — the book as Maier printed it, title page to Emblem L.
- **Order of the work** — the same fifty-one stations regrouped by the operation
  each one stages (`emblemdata.js` already tags every emblem: calcination,
  solution, separation, conjunction, putrefaction, congelation, fixation,
  digestion, fusion, fermentation, multiplication, projection), sequenced by
  **Ripley's twelve gates**. The tour then flies across the world following one
  operation, which is a genuinely different reading of the same terrain rather
  than a second copy of it.
- **Free explore** — no tour. The panel follows whichever station you stand in.

Rejected: building a second world laid out by process. It would double the
asset cost and, worse, it would imply the two orders are two places. They are
one place read two ways, and that is the interesting claim.

Consequence for the camera: on the process route consecutive stops can be
500 m apart, and a 500 m hop taken in two seconds is nauseating rather than
cinematic. `CameraDirector._flightTime()` now scales the handoff duration with
distance (base + d/110, capped at 7.5 s).

### 10. Commentary in colour-coded levels, not one blob of prose

**2026-09-04, at Ted's direction, following the HPin3D pattern.** The panel
carries **nine archetypes**, each with its own colour, so a reader can see at a
glance which register they are in without reading a paragraph to find out:

| archetype | colour | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| `motto` | near-black | Maier's motto, Latin and English |
| `epigram` | dark brown | Maier's own verse |
| `image` | grey | what Merian actually engraved |
| `discourse` | burnt orange | Maier's prose discourse |
| `dejong` | blue | H.M.E. de Jong's reading, with her page |
| `historical` | green | Craven, Tilton, Forshaw, Szulakowska… |
| `alchemical` | gold | the alchemical or hermetic text being cited |
| `myth` | violet | the classical or biblical story being alluded to |
| `register` | teal | one figure in four registers at once |
| `solve` | brass | the geometry, and what it refused to claim |

The `register` boxes are the distinctive ones and they come straight out of the
Claudiens dictionary's `registers` column: the same figure read *in the
laboratory*, *in the body*, *in the soul* and *in the heavens*. 100 of the
term-to-emblem links carry all four. Mean 12 boxes per station, range 6–25.

### 11. The fugues play

**2026-09-04.** *Atalanta Fugiens* is "emblems for the eyes, fugues for the
ears, epigrams for the intellect", and the world was dropping the third
register. `chiptune.js` already renders all fifty three-voice canons through an
NES-APU-style synth — two pulse channels and a triangle bass, one per canon
voice, which is exactly what the three tonal NES channels are for. The station
you are standing in now plays its own canon, looping, cycling the CODEXSYNTH
game palettes.

Off until asked (<kbd>M</kbd>, or `&sound=1`): a page that starts making noise
unbidden is a page people close. `fugues.json` and `chiptune.js` were vendored
into `site/` because they lived at absolute paths outside the published root.

### 12. Give the hand an instrument instead of guessing horizons

**2026-09-04.** The ask was "solve more plates by hand". The tempting way to do
that is for me to eyeball a horizon on each plate and write it into the
overrides. That is exactly the failure Phase 5 was built to stop: it produces
numbers that look measured and are not. (Proof it would have gone wrong: I
hand-computed the equal-height construction for Emblem XLII and got ny 0.42;
the actual construction, run properly on the same marked points, gives 0.5788.)

So instead: **the estimator, and the instrument for marking.**

- `tools/equal_height_horizon.py` — the Criminisi/Reid/Zisserman equal-height
  construction. For two figures of the same height on one ground plane, the
  line through their heads and the line through their feet meet on the horizon,
  *with no assumption about how tall anyone is*. With three or more the horizon
  is over-determined, fitted by least squares, and the residual is a real error
  bar. It reports where the horizon cuts each figure as a fraction of its
  height — those numbers must agree, and when they do not the plate is telling
  you the figures are not the same height or not on one plane.
- `review/serve.py` `POST /api/figures` — saves marks to `data/figures.json`,
  runs the construction, and writes the horizon into the overrides **only when
  asked**, because a mark is an observation and an override is a claim.
- The review app now has a click-to-mark canvas: click a head, then that
  figure's foot, repeat, and the horizon is drawn live.

This is the estimator PROPOSAL_PHASE6 §1 said the corpus needs and could not
have, because the extractor finds five figures across fifty-one plates. Marking
them by hand is the way round that, and it is now a two-click job per figure.
