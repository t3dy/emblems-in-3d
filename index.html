Phase 5 — Reconstruction

Rebuilding the spaces of Michael Maier's Atalanta Fugiens (1617) from each plate's own perspective construction, instead of guessing depth from where things sit on the page.

Overview The three examples All 51 plates Method What was wrong

Merian's engravings are ruled constructions. He worked from a horizon and one or two vanishing points, and the evidence is still in the picture: paving grids, wall courses, cornices, the lit doorway at the end of a vault. Earlier versions of this project ignored all of it and set each figure's depth with

depth = vertical_position − category_bias − 0.15·area

which contains no information about perspective at all. This phase replaces it with the plain pinhole relation for a camera of focal length f at eye height E looking at a flat ground plane:

Z = f · E / (y − horizon)

Every depth on this site comes from that and from nothing else, applied to where an element's mask touches the ground rather than to where its bounding box happens to be centred. Sizes follow from the same relation, so a card always subtends the angle it subtends in the engraving.

Setting the 3D camera to the same f and E then makes the whole thing testable: the reconstruction must reproject onto the plate it came from. That is the reprojection gate, and it is the check this project never had. Most procedural-graphics work has to invent its visual contract. Here there are 51 of them, printed in 1617.

How to read a reconstruction

Walk infirst person, spawning at the engraver's own eye height — for Emblem VIII that is 1.09 m, not a default 1.7 m, and standing up straight puts you in a different room.
Reprojection gatepress twice for the inverted overlay. Flat grey means the reconstruction registers with the engraving. Anything that still shows structure is a disagreement, and the page says so in words as well.
Open the bookslides every card between the flat plate and its solved depth, with the angle-preserving scale applied the whole way, so the composition stays registered as it opens. This is what a tunnel book actually does.
The notes panelevery number the scene was built from, and every place a number was assumed rather than measured.

Where the honesty is

Three of the 51 solves are hand-measured. The other 48 are automatic, and most of them are weak: only 10 reach a confidence of 0.25, and the reason is worth stating plainly — dense engraved hatching is thousands of short parallel lines, and it swamps a Hough-and-RANSAC vanishing-point search. The automatic pass is a first draft, not a result. The all-plates page shows every solve drawn back onto its engraving so you can see which ones are wrong, and the local review app turns those judgements into overrides the pipeline reads.

Wherever a quantity could not be measured, the record says ASSUMED and gives the reasoning. Two attempts to measure Emblem VIII's focal length failed; both failures are written down rather than quietly replaced with a plausible number.
