The pipeline

Five stages. The first three run offline in Python, where the computer vision already lives; the last two are the three.js runtime.

#stagewhat it doesoutput
1perspective solve Canny → Hough → separate the left- and right-leaning oblique segments → RANSAC over pairwise intersections for a vanishing point → horizon. Two opposed clusters also give the focal length through the orthogonality constraint f² = −(v₁−c)·(v₂−c); with only one, f is not recoverable from a single image and the record says ASSUMED. data/perspective.json
2ground contact + taxonomy For each extracted mask, the foot line — the lowest row carrying a real run of pixels, not a speckle — plus a confidence and the reason for it. And a kind from the five-way taxonomy below. data/elements.json
3web assets + rectified ground Self-contained plates, and for every usable solve an inverse-perspective warp of the region below the horizon into a top-down texture — the engraved pavement, laid back down as a floor. site/assets/
4assembly Architecture built or left on the backdrop; standing elements placed at their contact depth; attached elements projected onto surfaces as decals; ornament pinned where it cannot parallax. site/js/reconstruct.js
5reprojection gate Render from the station point, lay the source plate over it inverted at 50%. Flat grey means registered. the G key

The one relation everything rests on

For a pinhole camera of focal length f, at height E above a flat ground plane, looking horizontally, a point whose ground contact appears at image row y lies at depth

Z = f · E / (y − horizon)

and its lateral position and its world size follow from the same projection:

X = (x − W/2) · Z / f     size = pixels · Z / f

Two consequences make the whole method work. First, depth now comes from where an object touches the ground, so a tall figure and a short one standing on the same paving stone get the same depth — which the old bounding-box-centre heuristic could never do. Second, because size scales with Z at exactly the rate perspective shrinks it, a card pushed from 5 m to 500 m does not move on screen at all. It only changes how it parallaxes when you step sideways. That is what a tunnel book is. The previous renderer popped cards forward with no compensation, so every popped figure grew into a giant pasted over a small landscape.

Why the gate matters

Setting the 3D camera to the same f and E the scene was built from means the reconstruction must reproject onto the source plate. Any disagreement is a defect in the input — a wrong horizon, a bad foot line, an element filed under the wrong kind — rather than a matter of taste.

It is a real test, not a formality. On Emblem I it fails: the upper two-thirds go flat grey, and the bottom third, where a rectified "ground" has been laid over a bank of shrubs that is not a flat plane, keeps its structure. The page says so in words next to the scene rather than waiting for someone to notice.

It also has a limit worth naming. The gate tests registration, not support. Emblem VIII's egg rests on a bench, but its mask's lowest row is read as ground contact, so it is placed nearer than it really is — and it still reprojects perfectly. Passing the gate is necessary, not sufficient.

What is assumed, and where it says so

Focal length cannot be recovered from a single image with one vanishing point. Where it is assumed, the record says ASSUMED and gives the reasoning. Emblem VIII carries both failed attempts to measure it: the orthogonality constraint needs a second vanishing point that the plate does not provide, and a constrained search for the diagonal pavement's distance points found one weak cluster of eight segments implying a 110° field of view and a courtyard two metres deep. That result is recorded and rejected, not adopted.

The same discipline covers the reverse case. Emblem I's Boreas is drawn at roughly 1.8× human scale — 3.08 m against a 1.6 m viewpoint, measured from where the horizon crosses him. That is Merian monumentalising the wind-god, not a solver error, and it is written into the data as an anomaly to preserve so that no later pass can quietly normalise it away.
